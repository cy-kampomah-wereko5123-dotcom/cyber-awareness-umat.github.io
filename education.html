Understanding Sextortion and Cyberbullying

What is Sextortion?

Sextortion is a form of blackmail where someone threatens to share explicit images or videos of you unless you meet their demands, often for money or more content. It often starts with online interactions on social media or dating apps.

What is Cyberbullying?

Cyberbullying involves using digital platforms to harass, threaten, or humiliate someone. This can include spreading rumors, posting hurtful comments, or sharing private information without consent.

Impacts on Victims

These crimes can lead to severe emotional distress, anxiety, depression, academic decline, social isolation, and in extreme cases, self-harm or suicidal thoughts. Victims often feel shame and hesitate to seek help.

Prevention Strategies

For Sextortion

  • Never share explicit photos or videos, even with trusted people.
  • Use strong, unique passwords and enable two-factor authentication.
  • Be cautious of fast-developing online relationships and unknown friend requests.
  • Keep social media accounts private and limit personal information shared.
  • Cover your webcam and avoid clicking suspicious links.

Source: Ghana Internet Safety Foundation and FBI.

For Cyberbullying

  • Protect your privacy: Only accept friend requests from known people.
  • Think before posting: Avoid sharing sensitive information.
  • Block and report bullies on platforms.
  • Don't respond or retaliate to provoke further harassment.
  • Educate yourself on platform privacy settings.

Source: Cyber Security Authority (CSA) Ghana and UNICEF.

Online Privacy Tips

Protecting your online privacy is essential to preventing sextortion and cyberbullying. Here are some practical tips tailored for young adults in Ghana:

  • Use strong, unique passwords for each account and enable two-factor authentication to secure your profiles.
  • Set your social media accounts to private and regularly review privacy settings to control who sees your information.
  • Avoid sharing explicit images or videos, even in trusted relationships, as they can be misused.
  • Don't disclose personal details like your address, phone number, or exact location online.
  • Disable location services when not needed and be cautious with geotagged posts.
  • Never open unsolicited messages or click suspicious links to avoid phishing and malware.
  • Log out of accounts on shared devices and close unused accounts to minimize risks.
  • Use tools like parental controls or monitoring apps if sharing devices with family.

What to Do If You're a Victim

  • Don't pay or comply with demands – it often escalates.
  • Block the perpetrator and save all evidence (screenshots, messages).
  • Report to authorities: In Ghana, contact the Cyber Security Authority (CSA) at report@csa.gov.gh or Ghana Police Service.
  • Seek support: Talk to a trusted adult, counselor, or helpline like the National Helpline for Victims (contact campus counseling).
  • Report to the platform where it occurred (e.g., Facebook, Instagram).

Source: FBI, GISF, and CSA Ghana.
