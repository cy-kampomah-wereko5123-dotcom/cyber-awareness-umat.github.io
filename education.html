Understand the Terms

What Are These Crimes?

Digital crimes against personal dignity are more common than many realize. Knowing the terms is the first step to protecting yourself and others.

Sextortion

What is Sextortion?

Sextortion is a form of blackmail where someone threatens to share explicit images or videos of you unless you meet their demands — often money or more content. It typically starts through social media or dating apps and escalates quickly.

Cyberbullying

What is Cyberbullying?

Cyberbullying involves using digital platforms to harass, threaten, or humiliate someone. This can include spreading rumors, posting hurtful comments, sharing private information, or sustained harassment campaigns online.

Why It Matters

Impact on Victims

These crimes cause lasting harm that extends far beyond the digital world. Victims often suffer in silence due to shame and fear of judgment.

Mental Health

Emotional & Psychological

Severe anxiety, depression, PTSD, social isolation, and in extreme cases, self-harm or suicidal thoughts. Victims often feel shame and blame themselves.

Academic

Academic Decline

Inability to concentrate, frequent absences, declining performance, and in some cases, dropping out entirely due to ongoing harassment.

Social

Social Isolation

Withdrawal from friends and family, fear of judgment, and inability to trust others. Relationships suffer long-term damage.

Financial

Financial Loss

Many victims pay extortion demands to stop the threats, often losing significant sums with no guarantee the abuse will end.

Stay Safe

Prevention Strategies

Against Sextortion

  • Never share explicit photos or videos, even with trusted people.
  • Use strong, unique passwords and enable two-factor authentication.
  • Be cautious of fast-developing online relationships and unknown friend requests.
  • Keep social media accounts private and limit personal information shared.
  • Cover your webcam and avoid clicking suspicious links.

Source: Ghana Internet Safety Foundation & FBI

Against Cyberbullying

  • Only accept friend requests from people you know in real life.
  • Think before posting — avoid sharing sensitive personal information.
  • Block and report bullies on all platforms immediately.
  • Don't respond or retaliate; it often escalates the situation.
  • Educate yourself on privacy settings for every platform you use.

Source: Cyber Security Authority (CSA) Ghana & UNICEF

Protect Yourself

Online Privacy Tips

Practical tips tailored for young adults in Ghana to keep your digital life secure.

1

Strong Passwords & 2FA

Use strong, unique passwords for each account and enable two-factor authentication to secure your profiles.

2

Private Accounts

Set your social media accounts to private and regularly review privacy settings to control who sees your information.

3

No Explicit Content

Avoid sharing explicit images or videos even in trusted relationships — they can be misused against you.

4

Limit Personal Details

Don't disclose your address, phone number, or exact location online. This includes in bios and posts.

5

Location Services

Disable location services when not needed and be cautious with geotagged posts that reveal your whereabouts.

6

Suspicious Links

Never open unsolicited messages or click suspicious links — these are common phishing and malware vectors.

7

Shared Devices

Always log out of accounts on shared devices and close unused accounts to minimize your digital footprint.

Take Action

What to Do If You're a Victim

If you are experiencing sextortion or cyberbullying, know that you are not alone and there are clear steps you can take right now. Acting quickly matters — follow these steps in order.

🚨

In Immediate Danger or Crisis?

If you feel unsafe, threatened, or are in mental health crisis, call for help right now. Ghana emergency lines are free and available 24/7.

📞 191 📞 112 📧 [email protected]
1

Do NOT Pay or Comply

Never meet the demands — paying almost always leads to more demands, not freedom. Complying gives the perpetrator more power over you and does not guarantee the content will not be shared anyway. This is the most important rule.

2

Stop All Contact & Block

Immediately stop responding to the perpetrator. Block them on every platform they contacted you on. Do not delete your account yet — this could remove evidence needed for reporting.

3

Document & Preserve All Evidence

Before blocking, take screenshots of all messages, threats, usernames, profile links, and any images or videos they sent. Note the dates and times. Save everything — this is your evidence for authorities.

4

Report to Authorities in Ghana

File a report with the Cyber Security Authority (CSA) or the Ghana Police Service Cybercrime Unit. You can also report online.

🌐 csa.gov.gh/report 📧 [email protected] 📞 191 / 112
5

Report to the Platform Where It Happened

Every major platform has built-in tools to report abuse, non-consensual content, and blackmail. Reporting triggers account reviews and can lead to removal. Click below to go directly to each platform's reporting page.

6

Tell Someone You Trust

Shame and fear are tools perpetrators use to keep victims silent. Telling a trusted person — a friend, family member, lecturer, or counselor — is a brave and important step. You do not have to carry this alone. The UMaT Counseling Unit provides confidential support to all students.

7

Take Care of Your Mental Health

Being victimized online can cause anxiety, shame, depression, and trauma. These feelings are valid and real. Seeking professional mental health support is not weakness — it is how you recover and reclaim your life.

💚

Remember: It Is Never Your Fault

Whatever happened, the responsibility lies entirely with the perpetrator — not you. You deserve support, justice, and recovery. Many people have been through this and come out stronger. Reach out for help and take it one step at a time.

Get Help

Resources & Help Lines

You are not alone. Below is a comprehensive list of organizations, hotlines, and online tools available to support you — in Ghana and internationally. All resources are free unless noted.
