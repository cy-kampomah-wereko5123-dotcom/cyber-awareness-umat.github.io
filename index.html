Proposal Education & Resources

1.0 Executive Summary

This proposal outlines the production of a short narrative video (30-50 minutes) aimed at raising awareness about sextortion and cyberbullying among university students. The video will combine realistic drama with informational resources to highlight the psychological and social impacts of these digital crimes, promote empathy, and educate viewers on prevention and response strategies. It is intended for campus-wide distribution via social media, orientation programs, and student wellness portals.

2.0 Background & Rationale

Sextortion (sexual extortion) and cyberbullying are growing concerns in digital environments, particularly among young adults. According to recent studies, individuals aged 18–24 are at high risk of experiencing coercive online behaviors, which can lead to severe emotional distress, academic decline, and even self-harm. Many students are unaware of how to safely navigate these threats or where to seek help.

As digital natives, students are both the most vulnerable and the most influential audience for change. A visually engaging, peer-produced short film can effectively capture attention, foster discussion, and reduce stigma around reporting such incidents.

3.0 Project Objectives

  • To explain what sextortion and cyberbullying are in a clear and relatable manner.
  • To demonstrate common scenarios through short, dramatized scenes.
  • To highlight the emotional, academic, and social effects on victims.
  • To provide practical steps on prevention, response, and where to seek help.
  • To encourage empathy, digital responsibility, and peer support among students.

4.0 Target Audience

The primary target audience are internet users, particularly those aged 18–30. Secondary audiences include tertiary institution students, student leaders, lecturers, and campus counseling units.

5.0 Description of the Video Production

The proposed video will be a 30-50 minutes short film produced by the team. It will combine:

  • Short dramatized scenes showing realistic cases of sextortion and cyberbullying.
  • Narration or on-screen text explaining key messages.
  • Clear calls to action, such as reporting abuse and seeking support.

The tone will be serious but relatable, avoiding graphic content while still communicating the gravity of the issues.

5.1 Logline:

A final-year mathematics prodigy must solve the most critical equation of his life after a private moment is stolen and weaponized for blackmail, threatening to erase his perfect future.

Concept Teaser:

Tahiru's life is a model of elegant logic: he’s months from graduation, top of his class, and has secured a prestigious graduate position. His world is built on proof, predictability, and control. That ends when a single compromised video leads to a brutal sextortion demand.

Intrigue Angle:

We’ll witness a brilliant, analytical mind trained to solve complex theoretical problems confront a real-world crisis designed to exploit shame and silence. Will his logic save him, or will the pressure to maintain a flawless image destroy everything he’s built?

This short film explores the terrifying intersection of digital vulnerability, academic pressure, and the courage it takes to ask for help when you’ve always been the one with the answers.

6.0 Production Plan

6.1 Team Roles

  • Director/Scriptwriter: Sarah Thompson
  • Cinematographer: Henry Abban
  • Sound & Editor: Hussien Ofori and Ernest Dadzie
  • Actors: Team members and volunteers
  • Web Developers: Samuel Roland Asahin and Ampomah-wereko Kwame

6.2 Schedule

  • Pre-production (script, casting, location scouting): 1 week
  • Shooting: 2 weeks (on campus and private indoor locations)
  • Post-production (editing, sound, colour grading): 1 week
  • Review & final delivery: 4 days

6.3 Equipment & Resources

  • Camera: Smartphones or basic cameras to keep production cost-effective.
  • Audio: Lapel mics and boom pole
  • Editing: Adobe Premiere Pro (available on campus labs)
  • Locations: Dorm room, library, campus counselling center (permissions pending)

6.4 Publicity Strategy

  1. Leverage University Digital Infrastructure (e.g., SRC bill board)
  2. Collaborate with Support Services (e.g., Counseling Unit and SRC Welfare)
  3. Peer-to-Peer and Social Media Engagement
    • Teaser Campaign on WhatsApp: Since WhatsApp is widely used in Ghana, create short, intriguing teaser clips or posters featuring the logline ("A final-year math prodigy..."). Ask team members and friends to share these on their statuses and in departmental groups.
    • Instagram/TikTok Reels: Create short, visually engaging Reels focusing on the "intrigue angle" of the story rather than graphic content. Use trending audio but overlay text about the upcoming release.
    • Influencer Collaboration: Identify popular student influencers (e.g., SRC personalities, hall presidents, well-known students) to share the trailer or post a story about why they think this conversation is important.

7.0 Ethical Considerations

  • All actors will be informed of the sensitive content.
  • Script avoids graphics or triggering visual depictions.
  • Support resources will be displayed throughout and after the video.
  • Consent forms for all participants.
  • Advisor oversight to ensure accurate representation of psychological impacts.

8.0 Conclusion

Sextortion and cyberbullying are real and growing threats within university communities. This proposed short video production provides a practical, creative, and impactful approach to educating students and promoting safer online behavior. By using student voices and realistic scenarios, the project aims to create awareness, inspire responsibility, and contribute to a more secure digital environment on campus.
